Welcome to my Webiary

This is my composition-notebook blog — part field journal, part case file, part scrapbook of the adventures happening between builds and lesson plans.

I started keeping a webiary because the tidy version of my life never quite captured it — the bald eagles, the dogs with grudges, the classrooms full of kids signing their first line of code. This notebook is where the real stories live.

Use the tabs on the right (or swipe) to flip between sections — Teaching, Projects, Writing, and About.

Teaching

Teaching is where I spend most of my heart. Here's the work behind the work.

Deaf Kids Code — Tech Evangelist

I travel to schools to teach coding based on each student's individual skill level, building custom curriculum for diverse learning needs. I've represented Deaf Kids Code at hackathons and tech events around the world, teaching coding, AI, and digital creativity entirely in American Sign Language.

CNCF Deaf & Hard of Hearing Working Group

I contribute to accessibility efforts across open-source communities, and previously served as Co-Chair. I help develop best-practice documents for conference organizers, maintainers, and attendees — focused on inclusive communication and equitable participation at tech events. I now also co-lead the CNCF Deep Roots Working Group, helping lead sign language accessibility efforts across the cloud native community.

ASL Instructor, American Society for Deaf Children (ASDC)

I teach American Sign Language through ASDC programs, helping families and learners build the language skills that open doors — the same doors I want every Deaf kid to walk through with confidence.

Case File DKC2025: Deaf Kids Code Goes Global

Teaching Deaf Kids Code ASL

From Cleveland to India, I've presented Deaf Kids Code to students virtually all over the world — teaching AI and code through ASL, sparking curiosity and agency in Deaf youth. Every session is a new entry logged in our mission for inclusive tech education. Different time zones, different classrooms, same message: you belong in this field, and your language belongs here too.

Filed: January 2025

I Got Chased by a Bald Eagle (and That's Just the Beginning)

Field Notes Missouri Nature Did Not Consent

On the Meramec River in Missouri, I was just vibing in a canoe when the sky opened up — and somewhere between the wind, the low trees, and a very unimpressed bald eagle, I found myself paddling for my life. There were spiders. There were branches I did not see coming. There was a moment I will only describe as "negotiating with a bird of prey." I made it back to shore with a story, a few new gray hairs, and a deep respect for anything with talons.

The Dalmatian Named Sprinkles (Who Wasn't So Sweet)

Throwback Inglewood, CA Kid Chaos

Age 9. Inglewood, California. One very sweetly-named dalmatian who turned out to have zero interest in living up to it. One chase down the block that felt, at the time, like the final scene of an action movie. One very confused nine-year-old who has never fully trusted a polka-dotted anything since. Legend says I'm still running.
